RangerTrak Tour Guide

Tour v1, for RangerTrak 0.99.81-alpha or later (the app’s footer shows the version).

If you’ve some time, use this tour to guide your exploration of RangerTrak.

You will run a small radio net from a quiet first look to a busy afternoon, an afternoon with no Internet, and the paperwork after.

Nothing here uses real people or real places beyond a national park, and nothing you do can reach anyone else. The story is fictional, and RangerTrak has no link to the National Park Service.

Start anywhere, and see how you get on. Try module 1 on your own before you meet with your team, so the first session isn’t spent finding buttons. Bookmark this page, and come back when you have more time: every module stands on its own.

Break off and come back as you wish: your data stays as you left it. One exception: on an iPhone, iPad, or a Mac using Safari, the browser can clear it after about a week unused, so install RangerTrak (T4.1) and back up (T8.7) before a long break.

Each module’s Setup says what it needs from earlier ones; otherwise, skip around freely.

Solo or crew. Every module works alone: you can read the radio calls aloud to yourself and log them.

It is more realistic with two to four people: one person is the scribe at the keyboard; the others act as field teams, reading calls off the cards to the scribe, ideally over real radios. Switch positions, op periods & discuss!

What you need.

  • A laptop or tablet with Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. This is the scribe’s device.
  • A phone for modules 6 and 7 (any phone with a browser).
  • Pen and paper for whoever plays a field team.
  • For module 6, the Board: Wi-Fi that your laptop and phone share. A second laptop is welcome but not needed.

How the checks work. Each module opens with its setup, goals and situation, then steps. Steps marked T are tests: do the thing, and it should happen.

Items marked Q are questions: we want your opinion. Each has its own number, such as T3.4 (module 3, test 4). When something is wrong, odd or slow, write the number down with one line about it. That is the whole feedback form.

Like a paper script? Print the Tester sheet (or save it as a PDF): every ID in one list, with Pass, Fail and Note columns.

If something breaks, carry on: the next step rarely depends on it. Note the number, and if you can, press AAR/214 at the top of the page right then (module 1 shows you how).

Module 1 First look

Setup

A standard browser with Internet. Solo.

Goals

Find out what RangerTrak is for, and get your bearings before you rely on it.

Situation

You’ve heard RangerTrak is a radio log that works with no Internet. Before trusting it, you look round somebody else’s mission.

  • T1.1 Optional. Open rangertrak.com and review its pages’ major content: What it does, Field Guide, Blog. Nothing there is needed for the tour or in the field, but it is a different view, and reveals some less-obvious features. #
  • T1.3 Press Open RangerTrak (or go to rangertrak.org). The Entry page loads, and a card offers a demo. #
  • T1.4 Load the Grand Canyon, South Rim demo from the Entry card, or Help → Start → Load a demo. A roster and about thirty radio log entries appear. #
  • T1.5 Explore the Radio Log, Map, and Rangers pages using the links at the top. Each shows different aspects of the demo mission; the map shows markers along the South Rim. #
  • T1.6 Press Help at the top of any page. It explains that screen; its link at the bottom opens the full Help page (FAQ, Your data, After mission, Feedback); look round that too. #
  • T1.7 Press Full view at the top of Entry. More controls appear (Attach, From / To station). Press it repeatedly to switch in and out of 'Simple view’. Switching never affects the data. #
  • T1.8 Optional. Press AAR/214 at the top of any page. Use it for your After Action Review notes on the current incident or RangerTrak — or update your own ICS-214 activity log. Type info into each now and save. You return to where you were before. #
  • T1.9 Optional. Close the browser completely, restart the device if you like, then open rangertrak.org again. The Grand Canyon demo is still there. Your data is preserved in the browser’s long-term memory through an incident, and through browser and RangerTrak updates (module 8 covers the one exception). #
  • T1.10 Optional. Load another demo and look through the pages again. #
  • Q1.1 Do you feel you understand basically what RangerTrak is for? What questions do you have? Is RangerTrak a good name? #
  • Q1.2 How does your team call in their reports: using a call sign, a credential ID, a position title or team name? Do you typically specify the tactical destination? #
  • Q1.3 Was anything on the app’s first screen confusing or unexplained? #

Skills learned: load a demo; move between Entry, Radio Log, Map and Rangers; use Help, on each screen and the full page; switch Full view and Simple view; save an AAR/214 note.

Module 2 Your first radio call

Setup

Verify the Grand Canyon demo is loaded. Solo, or a partner to read the calls.

Goals

Take a check-in and a call for help from a field team, and keep track of which calls still need action.

Situation

A team on the rim trail checks in, then finds a hiker with a twisted ankle.

Read each call aloud (or have your field team read it), then log it on the Entry page. Pick any ranger from the demo roster for the Ranger ID.

CardCallLog it as
C2.1"Net control, on the rim trail at the Village. All four present, starting our sweep."Click the Village on Entry’s small map. Status: whatever means OK
C2.2"Net control, a hiker with a twisted ankle a few hundred meters east of us. We’re with her."Click a bit east. A status that means needs attention. Notes: the call
C2.3"Net control, ankle is wrapped, she’s walking out with us. Cancel the request."Same spot. Back to OK
  • T2.1 Typing part of a ranger’s name, ID or call sign in Ranger ID offers matches. #
  • T2.2 Clicking Entry’s small map sets the location straight away (and copies it, in case you want it). #
  • T2.3 Each saved entry gets a message number, such as A031, and it shows on the Radio Log. #
  • T2.4 On the Radio Log, click the small box at the left of C2.2’s entry. It cycles ✓, →, ! and back to empty. #
  • T2.5 Your three entries are on the Map, near the Village. #
  • Q2.1 Time yourself on C2.3. How long from hearing the call to saved? Did anything slow you down? #
  • Q2.2 Were the status names the words your team would use? (They can be renamed on Mission; you will in module 4.) #

Skills learned: log an entry on Entry with a Ranger ID, location and status; find its message number on the Radio Log; mark it ✓, → or !.

Module 3 Where exactly?

Setup

The Grand Canyon demo loaded. Solo or pairs.

Goals

Write down where a team is, whatever kind of map or GPS they are reading from.

Situation

Radio calls come in whatever format the caller’s map uses.

Entry has a small switcher above the coordinate boxes: pick the format the caller used, type, and the others fill in by themselves. Log each card as a new entry, then check where it lands.

CardFormatCallShould land at
C3.1Decimal degrees"36.0571 north, 112.1438 west"Bright Angel trailhead
C3.2Degrees, decimal minutes"36 degrees 03.702 north, 112 degrees 06.468 west"Mather Point
C3.3MGRS"12 Sierra Uniform Echo, 90980 91292"Hermits Rest, the west end of the rim road
C3.4UTM"Zone 12 S, easting 399409, northing 3991823"Yavapai Point
C3.5Maidenhead"Grid square DM36wb"Somewhere in a box a few km across around the Village
C3.6Place name (needs Internet)"Desert View Watchtower"The far east end of the rim road, about 25 km from the Village
  • T3.1 to T3.6: each card lands where the last column says. (One test per card: C3.1 is T3.1, and so on.) #
  • T3.7 After typing C3.3, the other formats below the switcher show the same point (decimal degrees close to 36.0601, −112.2105). #
  • T3.8 On the Radio Log, select the six entries from this module, then open Map. You can show just those. #
  • T3.9 On the Radio Log, double-click the notes of one of your entries, change them and press Enter. The change is kept; its message number and time logged stay as they were. #
  • T3.10 Turn off Wi-Fi (hint, try airplane mode!) and try C3.6 again. The app says the lookup needs Internet, and the coordinate boxes still work. #
  • T3.11 Load the Montecito debris flows (large) demo (about 400 entries). The Radio Log and Map stay usable. Load Grand Canyon again afterwards. #
  • Q3.1 Which format does your team actually use on the air? Was it easy to find on the switcher? #
  • Q3.2 With 400 entries, what felt slow, if anything? #

Skills learned: enter a location in decimal degrees, degrees and decimal minutes, MGRS, UTM, Maidenhead or a place name with the format switcher; edit an entry; show selected Radio Log entries on Map.

Module 4 Set up your own exercise

Setup

Any text editor, for the roster file. Solo.

Goals

Get ready the night before: who is coming, when and where, and what everyone is called on the radio.

Situation

The park has asked your team to sweep the rim trail on Saturday. You have a roster in a spreadsheet and an hour at the kitchen table.

Save these lines as rim-roster.csv (any text editor will do):

Name,Call sign,Cell,Team,Status
Avery Lindqvist,WZ0RIM,555-0101,Hermit,Active
Sam Okafor,WZ0SAM,555-0102,Hermit,Active
Jo Whitecloud,WZ0JO,555-0103,Mather,Active
Riley Park,,555-0104,Mather,Active
Morgan Ellis,WZ0OLD,555-0105,Mather,Retired
  • T4.1 Install RangerTrak now, before your own data goes in. Press Install in the header (on an iPhone or iPad: Share, then Add to Home Screen). It opens in its own window with its own icon; use that from now on, and module 8’s offline test counts on it. On an iPhone or iPad the installed app starts empty, apart from Safari’s copy; that is expected. #
  • T4.2 On Entry, choose Start a real mission. RangerTrak lists what will be cleared and lets you choose. Keep your AAR notes (you need them in module 9); the log empties. #
  • T4.3 On Mission, open the Danger zone at the bottom. Look, don’t press. Radio Log and Rangers each have one too, and between them they hold every action that resets, replaces or clears your data at once. Anything that replaces data asks first and says what goes, as Start a real mission just did. #
  • T4.4 Hover the icon at the left of the status pill in the header and set the mission to Exercise. #
  • T4.5 On Mission, name it Rim Sweep and set an operational period of today, 09:00 to 15:00. A small Saved note appears; there is no Save button. #
  • T4.6 Set the default location to the Village, 36.0544, −112.1401. New entries now start there. #
  • T4.7 Under Operational period, This station logs as is A. #
  • T4.8 Rename one status to your team’s word for it. The Radio Log uses the new name. #
  • T4.9 In Tactical call signs, add Village Base, Hermit, Mather and Desert View. #
  • T4.10 On Rangers, Import, export & print → Import a list… and pick rim-roster.csv. The preview guesses which column is which; fix any wrong guess. #
  • T4.11 RangerTrak offers to skip Morgan Ellis (Retired). Four people arrive. #
  • T4.12 Add a walk-up by hand: Dana Cho, no call sign. It saves without a Save button. #
  • T4.13 Use Undo last import, then import again. The roster goes back and comes back; Dana is handled the way the undo message says. #
  • Q4.1 Which setup step would you most likely forget on the night before? Did the app nudge you about it? #
  • Q4.2 Did the column-matching step make sense for your real roster’s headings? #

Keep paper too. For a real event or a serious exercise, keep a paper ICS-309 log alongside RangerTrak, written as you go. Today you can skip it.

Skills learned: install RangerTrak; Start a real mission; set Exercise, the operational period, default location and station letter; rename a status; add Tactical call signs; import a roster on Rangers, and Undo last import.

Module 5 Run the net

Setup

The mission from module 4, in Full view. Best with a crew: one scribe, everyone else reads the cards in order, as fast as the scribe can keep up and then a little faster.

Goals

Keep up with a busy net: two teams, a lost hiker, a formal request for help, a photo and a change of radio operator.

Situation

Saturday, 09:00. Teams Hermit and Mather head out along the rim.

Solo, you play all three seats: read each card aloud, then log it.

C5.2Mather"Village Base, Mather. At Mather Point, starting west."
C5.3Hermit"Village Base, Hermit. Lost hiker, male, about 70, confused, at the Abyss overlook. Request a carry-out team."
C5.4Village BaseScribe sends a formal message: to Park Dispatch, "Request carry-out team to the Abyss overlook, one adult male, conscious, disoriented." (log as an ICS-213)
C5.5Mather"Village Base, Mather. Radio check, you’re weak but readable."
C5.6Hermit"Village Base, Hermit. Taking a photo of the trail washout for you." (attach any photo)
C5.7Mather"Village Base, Mather. Yavapai Point, all clear, continuing."
C5.8Hermit"Village Base, Hermit. Carry-out team arrived, patient handed over."
C5.9Mather"Village Base, Mather. Shift change, Jo is handing the radio to Riley."
  • T5.1 Picking Hermit in Ranger ID fills in the operator’s call sign. #
  • T5.2 C5.4: fill ICS-213 addressed to. The message appears on the Messages page. #
  • T5.3 C5.6: Attach (on a phone, Take photo / video). The entry shows a 📎 1 chip; hovering it on the Radio Log shows the picture. #
  • T5.4 C5.9: on Mission, change who is operating Mather. Earlier Mather entries keep their old operator. #
  • T5.5 Press AAR/214, choose ICS-214 line, and log "09:40 radio relay at Yavapai weak" with the time set back to 09:40. #
  • T5.6 Mark C5.3 ! and C5.8 ✓ on the Radio Log. #
  • Q5.1 Scribe: where did you fall behind? Field teams: how long did you wait? #
  • Q5.2 What would you want on the screen at 09:30 that isn’t there? #

Skills learned: fill in an ICS-213 message; Attach a photo; change who is operating a team on Mission; add an ICS-214 line from AAR/214; mark entries ✓ and ! on the Radio Log.

Module 6 The Board

Setup

The scribe’s laptop and a phone on the same Wi-Fi. A second laptop is welcome, and closer to a real command post.

Goals

Let the incident commander and others at the command post follow the log on their own screens, without crowding the scribe.

Situation

The incident commander wants to watch the log without standing behind the scribe.

  • T6.1 Start the RangerTrak Board program on the scribe’s laptop, or on a second one (Help → FAQ, Can several people watch the same log at once?). It prints an address and a QR code. #
  • T6.2 On the scribe’s device, Mission → RangerTrak Board card: turn on publishing, enter the address, set a viewers’ password, and press Test connection. It says it can connect, or gives one sentence on why not. #
  • T6.3 On a phone on the same Wi-Fi, scan the QR code and enter the password. The Rim Sweep log appears, read-only, and a new entry from the scribe shows up within a few seconds. #
  • T6.4 Close the Board program. Within a minute the phone says it has lost the Board, and the last reports stay on screen. #
  • Q6.1 Who on your team would set this up, and would they manage on their own? #
  • Q6.2 What would your commander want on the Board that isn’t there? #

Skills learned: start the RangerTrak Board program; turn on publishing in Mission’s RangerTrak Board card with a viewers’ password; Test connection; watch the Board from a phone.

Module 7 Reports from the field

Setup

optional. A phone with a browser, as well as the scribe’s laptop.

Goals

Take reports from teams by phone as well as by radio, and share the work with a second scribe.

Situation

The Mather team has phones with RangerTrak on them. Their reports can reach you without the radio, and a second scribe is coming to share the load.

  • T7.1 On the phone, open rangertrak.org, install it, and choose field mode on the welcome panel. Only Entry and Help appear. #
  • T7.2 Log two reports on the phone, then Send my reports. The phone’s share sheet opens; send it to the scribe’s device (email, a message to yourself, AirDrop or Quick Share). #
  • T7.3 On the scribe’s device, Radio Log → Load Report Packet. Two entries arrive with the scribe’s next message numbers. Loading the same file again adds nothing. #
  • T7.4 On the scribe’s device, Hand over to another scribe, with a passphrase. On a second device that already has entries of its own, Merge in. It shows what it would add before it does. #
  • T7.5 The second device chooses Log alongside as station B. Its new entries are numbered B001 and up. #
  • Q7.1 Would your field teams actually use Send my reports, or stay on the radio? Why? #
  • Q7.2 Was the difference between Take over and Log alongside clear? #

Skills learned: use field mode; Send my reports; Load Report Packet on the Radio Log; Hand over to another scribe; Merge in; Log alongside as station B.

Module 8 Lights out

Setup

The mission from module 5, with Internet for T8.1. Solo.

Goals

Keep the log going when the Internet goes down, and get it back if the laptop is lost.

Situation

10:15. The cell tower at the Village goes down, and the scribe’s laptop is on battery. This is what RangerTrak is for, so test it the hard way.

  • T8.1 While still online: on Map, switch the base layer to OpenTopoMap, zoom to the Village and press Save this area for offline use. #
  • T8.2 Flip the switch to the Alternative map once, then back. #
  • T8.3 Turn off Wi-Fi and mobile data. Close RangerTrak completely and open it again. It opens, with Rim Sweep and every entry still there. #
  • T8.4 Log two more Rim Sweep calls offline, in coordinates. They save and number as usual. #
  • T8.5 The Map still shows the Village in detail at the zoom you saved; further out or deeper in, it says what it is missing rather than going blank. #
  • T8.6 Turn the network back on. On Mission, Data safety shows the storage status; accept if it offers to protect your data. #
  • T8.7 Back up mission with a passphrase you will remember. Two files are saved, the backup and its attachments zip (for the C5.6 photo). Keep them to yourself; we only ask for them if we need to troubleshoot. #
  • T8.8 Load the Grand Canyon demo over Rim Sweep (yes, really). Then Restore mission with both files. A wrong passphrase gives a clear message; the right one brings back Rim Sweep, the photo and all. #
  • Q8.1 Before today, did you know your browser could throw away site data? Did the Data safety card explain it well enough? #
  • Q8.2 Where would your team keep the backup file during a real mission? #

Skills learned: Save this area for offline use on Map; log offline; check Data safety on Mission; Back up mission with a passphrase; Restore mission.

Module 9 After the mission

Setup

The mission you ran in modules 5 to 8. Solo.

Goals

Finish the paperwork: the log, messages and map for the record, and lessons learned for next time.

Situation

15:00. The teams are home and the coffee is on. The paperwork is the part people remember, so it should be the easy part.

  • T9.1 Radio Log → Print ICS-309 log. It is stamped EXERCISE, shows message numbers, and has your ✓ and ! marks in the first column. #
  • T9.2 Print C5.4’s ICS-213 from the Messages page. It carries its message number in the top margin. #
  • T9.3 On Map, Print map. The sheet shows the rim, the markers and the EXERCISE stamp. #
  • T9.4 Open AAR notes. Your T1.8 note and the 09:40 ICS-214 line are both there. Add an area, a recommendation and an owner to one incident note. #
  • T9.5 Export ICS-214 fills in FEMA’s form with the 09:40 line and offers suggestions from the journal. #
  • T9.6 Review and send brings your RangerTrak notes to Help → Feedback, for you to read before anything is sent. Sending makes a public GitHub issue, so keep real names and mission data out. Send them, or don’t: either is a pass. #
  • Q9.1 Which printout would your coordinator or served agency actually want? Which would they not? #
  • Q9.2 Teach one thing from this tour to someone who hasn’t seen RangerTrak. What did you pick, and what did they ask? #

Skills learned: Print ICS-309 log; print an ICS-213 from Messages; Print map; add to AAR notes; Export ICS-214; Review and send.
