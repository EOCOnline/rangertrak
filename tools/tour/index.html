Take RangerTrak for a Shift

Tour v1, for RangerTrak 0.99.81-alpha or later (the version is at the foot of the app’s Help page).

In about three hours, spread over as many sittings as you like, you will run a small radio net from a quiet first look to a busy afternoon, an afternoon with no Internet, and the paperwork after. Nothing here uses real people or real places beyond a national park, and nothing you do can reach anyone else. The story is fictional, and RangerTrak has no link to the National Park Service.

Start anywhere, and see how you get on. Try module 1 on your own before you meet with your team, so the first session isn’t spent finding buttons. Bookmark this page, and come back when you have more time: every module stands on its own.

Solo or crew. Every module works alone: you read the radio calls aloud to yourself and log them. It is better with two to four people. One person is the scribe at the keyboard; the others are field teams, reading calls off the cards at the scribe’s pace (or a little faster, which is the point).

What you need.

  • A laptop or tablet with Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari. This is the scribe’s device.
  • A phone for modules 6 and 7 (any phone with a browser).
  • Pen and paper for whoever plays a field team.
  • For module 6, the Board: Wi-Fi that your laptop and phone share. A second laptop is welcome but not needed.

How the checks work. Each module is a short story, then steps. Steps marked T are tests: do the thing, and it should happen. Items marked Q are questions: we want your opinion. Each has its own number, such as T3.4 (module 3, test 4). When something is wrong, odd or slow, write the number down with one line about it. That is the whole feedback form.

Like paper? Print the Tester sheet (or save it as a PDF): every ID in one list, with Pass, Fail and Note columns.

If something breaks, carry on: the next step rarely depends on it. Note the number, and if you can, press AAR/214 at the top of the page right then (module 1 shows you how).

Modules 1 to 3 use the built-in demo; from module 4 on, you run your own small exercise. Module 5 is the busy one, and the best to do with friends.

Module 1 — First look (15 min, solo)

You have heard RangerTrak is a radio log that works with no Internet. Before you trust it with anything, you want a look round: the website first, then the app, full of somebody else’s mission.

  • T1.1 Optional. Open rangertrak.com and spend two minutes on its menu: What it does, Field Guide, Blog. Nothing there is needed for the tour or in the field, but it is a different view, and it points out features you might not find on your own. #
  • T1.2 Open the Field Guide and read What RangerTrak does. It says what the app is for, in a few minutes’ reading. #
  • T1.3 Press Open RangerTrak (or go to rangertrak.org). The Entry page loads, and a card offers a demo. #
  • T1.4 Load the Grand Canyon, South Rim demo from the Entry card, or Help → Start → Load a demo. A roster and about thirty radio log entries appear. #
  • T1.5 Visit the Radio Log, Map, and Rangers pages using the links at the top. Each shows the demo mission; the map shows markers along the South Rim. #
  • T1.6 Press Guide at the top of any page. It explains that screen; close it again. #
  • T1.7 Press Full view at the top of Entry. More controls appear (Attach, From / To station). Press it again to go back to Simple view. Nothing in the log changes. #
  • T1.8 Press AAR/214 at the top of any page. Use it for your thoughts on the current incident or exercise, or on RangerTrak itself, or to add lines to your own ICS-214 activity log. Try it now: type "T1.8 test note", mark it RangerTrak, save. You stay on the page you were on. #
  • T1.9 Optional. Close the browser completely, restart the device if you like, then open rangertrak.org again. The Grand Canyon demo is still there. Your data stays through restarts, logging off, browser updates and RangerTrak updates (module 8 covers the one exception). #
  • T1.10 Optional. Load another demo (Vashon Island or the State fair, say) and look through the menu items. Load Grand Canyon again before module 2. #
  • Q1.1 After the website and a few minutes in the app, could you tell what RangerTrak is for? What would you have called it? #
  • Q1.2 Was anything on the app’s first screen confusing or unexplained? #

You can now: find your way round the website and the app, load a demo, and leave yourself a note.

Module 2 — Your first radio call (15 min, solo or pairs)

Still in the Grand Canyon demo, a team checks in. Read each call aloud (or have your field team read it), then log it on the Entry page. Pick any ranger from the demo roster for the Ranger ID.

CardCallLog it as
C2.1"Net control, on the rim trail at the Village. All four present, starting our sweep."Click the Village on Entry’s small map. Status: whatever means OK
C2.2"Net control, a hiker with a twisted ankle a few hundred meters east of us. We’re with her."Click a bit east. A status that means needs attention. Notes: the call
C2.3"Net control, ankle is wrapped, she’s walking out with us. Cancel the request."Same spot. Back to OK
  • T2.1 Typing part of a ranger’s name, ID or call sign in Ranger ID offers matches. #
  • T2.2 Clicking Entry’s small map sets the location straight away (and copies it, in case you want it). #
  • T2.3 Each saved entry gets a message number, such as A031, and it shows on the Radio Log. #
  • T2.4 On the Radio Log, click the small box at the left of C2.2’s entry. It cycles ✓, →, ! and back to empty. #
  • T2.5 Your three entries are on the Map, near the Village. #
  • Q2.1 Time yourself on C2.3. How long from hearing the call to saved? Did anything slow you down? #
  • Q2.2 Were the status names the words your team would use? (They can be renamed on Mission; you will in module 4.) #

You can now: log a radio call, and mark it handled.

Module 3 — Where exactly? (20 min, solo or pairs)

Radio calls come in whatever format the caller’s map uses. Entry has a small switcher above the coordinate boxes: pick the format the caller used, type, and the others fill in by themselves. Log each card as a new entry, then check where it lands.

  • T3.7 After typing C3.3, the other formats below the switcher show the same point (decimal degrees close to 36.0601, −112.2105). #
  • T3.8 On the Radio Log, select the six entries from this module, then open Map. You can show just those. #
  • T3.9 Turn off Wi-Fi and try C3.6 again. The app says the lookup needs Internet, and the coordinate boxes still work. #
  • T3.10 Load the Montecito debris flows (large) demo (about 400 entries). The Radio Log and Map stay usable. Load Grand Canyon again afterwards. #
  • Q3.1 Which format does your team actually use on the air? Was it easy to find on the switcher? #
  • Q3.2 With 400 entries, what felt slow, if anything? #

You can now: take a position in six formats and see it on the map.

  • T4.12 Add a walk-up by hand: Dana Cho, no call sign. It saves without a Save button. #
  • T4.13 Use Undo last import, then import again. The roster goes back and comes back; Dana is handled the way the undo message says. #
  • Q4.1 Which setup step would you most likely forget on the night before? Did the app nudge you about it? #
  • Q4.2 Did the column-matching step make sense for your real roster’s headings? #

Keep paper too. For a real event or a serious exercise, keep a paper ICS-309 log alongside RangerTrak, written as you go. Today you can skip it.

You can now: set up a mission from a spreadsheet, the way you would before a real one.

Module 5 — Run the net (25 min, best with a crew)

Saturday, 09:00. Teams Hermit and Mather head out along the rim. One person scribes; everyone else reads calls from the cards, in order, as fast as the scribe can keep up and then a little faster. Solo? Read them aloud and keep a timer running. Switch to Full view first.

Solo, you play all three seats: read each card aloud, then log it.

You can now: run a two-team net with a formal message, a photo and a shift change.

Module 6 — The Board (20 min, your laptop and a phone)

The incident commander wants to watch the log without standing behind the scribe. The RangerTrak Board is a small, private mini-website for the command post, run by a program on a command-post laptop. It shows a read-only copy of the log to anyone there with the viewers’ password: commander, planning, the served agency’s liaison. The Board program can run on the scribe’s own laptop, with your phone as the viewer. A second laptop works too, and is closer to a real command post.

  • T6.1 Start the RangerTrak Board program on the scribe’s laptop, or on a second one (Help → FAQ, Can several people watch the same log at once?). It prints an address and a QR code. #
  • T6.2 On the scribe’s device, Mission → RangerTrak Board card: turn on publishing, enter the address, set a viewers’ password, and press Test connection. It says it can connect, or gives one sentence on why not. #
  • T6.3 On a phone on the same Wi-Fi, scan the QR code and enter the password. The Rim Sweep log appears, read-only, and a new entry from the scribe shows up within a few seconds. #
  • T6.4 Close the Board program. Within a minute the phone says it has lost the Board, and the last reports stay on screen. #
  • Q6.1 Who on your team would set this up, and would they manage on their own? #
  • Q6.2 What would your commander want on the Board that isn’t there? #

You can now: put the log on the screens at the command post.

Module 7 — Reports from the field (optional, 20 min, two devices)

The Mather team has phones with RangerTrak on them. Their reports can reach you without the radio, and a second scribe is coming to share the load.

  • T7.1 On the phone, open rangertrak.org, install it, and choose field mode on the welcome panel. Only Entry and Help appear. #
  • T7.2 Log two reports on the phone, then Send my reports. The phone’s share sheet opens; send it to the scribe’s device (email, a message to yourself, AirDrop or Quick Share). #
  • T7.3 On the scribe’s device, Radio Log → Load Report Packet. Two entries arrive with the scribe’s next message numbers. Loading the same file again adds nothing. #
  • T7.4 On the scribe’s device, Hand over to another scribe, with a passphrase. On a second device that already has entries of its own, Merge in. It shows what it would add before it does. #
  • T7.5 The second device chooses Log alongside as station B. Its new entries are numbered B001 and up. #
  • Q7.1 Would your field teams actually use Send my reports, or stay on the radio? Why? #
  • Q7.2 Was the difference between Take over and Log alongside clear? #

You can now: take reports from field phones and share the log with a second scribe.

Module 8 — Lights out (20 min, solo)

10:15. The cell tower at the Village goes down, and the scribe’s laptop is on battery. This is what RangerTrak is for, so test it the hard way.

  • T8.1 While still online: on Map, switch the base layer to OpenTopoMap, zoom to the Village and press Save this area for offline use. #
  • T8.2 Flip the switch to the Alternative map once, then back. #
  • T8.3 Turn off Wi-Fi and mobile data. Close RangerTrak completely and open it again. It opens, with Rim Sweep and every entry still there. #
  • T8.4 Log two more Rim Sweep calls offline, in coordinates. They save and number as usual. #
  • T8.5 The Map still shows the Village in detail at the zoom you saved; further out or deeper in, it says what it is missing rather than going blank. #
  • T8.6 Turn the network back on. On Mission, Data safety shows the storage status; accept if it offers to protect your data. #
  • T8.7 Back up mission with a passphrase you will remember. Two files are saved, the backup and its attachments zip (for the C5.6 photo). Keep them to yourself; we only ask for them if we need to troubleshoot. #
  • T8.8 Load the Grand Canyon demo over Rim Sweep (yes, really). Then Restore mission with both files. A wrong passphrase gives a clear message; the right one brings back Rim Sweep, the photo and all. #
  • Q8.1 Before today, did you know your browser could throw away site data? Did the Data safety card explain it well enough? #
  • Q8.2 Where would your team keep the backup file during a real mission? #

You can now: keep logging with no Internet, and get a mission back after losing it.

Module 9 — After the mission (15 min, solo)

15:00. The teams are home and the coffee is on. The paperwork is the part people remember, so it should be the easy part.

  • T9.1 Radio Log → Print ICS-309 log. It is stamped EXERCISE, shows message numbers, and has your ✓ and ! marks in the first column. #
  • T9.2 Print C5.4’s ICS-213 from the Messages page. It carries its message number in the top margin. #
  • T9.3 On Map, Print map. The sheet shows the rim, the markers and the EXERCISE stamp. #
  • T9.4 Open AAR notes. Your T1.8 note and the 09:40 ICS-214 line are both there. Add an area, a recommendation and an owner to one incident note. #
  • T9.5 Export ICS-214 fills in FEMA’s form with the 09:40 line and offers suggestions from the journal. #
  • T9.6 Review and send brings your RangerTrak notes to Help → Feedback, for you to read before anything is sent. Sending makes a public GitHub issue, so keep real names and mission data out. Send them, or don’t: either is a pass. #
  • Q9.1 Which printout would your coordinator or served agency actually want? Which would they not? #
  • Q9.2 Teach one thing from this tour to someone who hasn’t seen RangerTrak. What did you pick, and what did they ask? #

You can now: close out a mission with the forms your agency expects.
